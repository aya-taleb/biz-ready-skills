# Job spec: offer

Written before looking at any other offer skill.

## The job

A founder has a product and a price. Buyers still say no, for reasons the founder can
name: too expensive, not sure it works, not now, I'd have to change how I do things. The
offer is everything around the product that answers those reasons: what is included,
what they get extra, what happens if it does not work, why decide now, and what it is
called. A good offer makes the yes easier without lying and without giving the margin away.

## Who runs it

Online and local founders who have at least a rough price. Usually after competitors (the
complaints), customer-discovery or the consumer panel (the objections), and pricing.

## What a good result has

1. The objections, ranked, each with where it came from (interviews, panel, complaints).
   Real sources first; simulated ones labeled as simulated.
2. For each top objection, the part of the offer that answers it. Every piece earns its
   place by answering an objection. Nothing added because "offers have bonuses".
3. The cost of each piece to the business, per sale, so the founder sees what the offer
   does to the margin. If costs are missing, it says so and points to founder-cfo.
4. A guarantee the business can actually afford and honor, with its terms written plainly.
5. Urgency or scarcity only when it is true (a real cohort size, a real date, real
   capacity). Never a fake countdown or a "was" price that was never charged.
6. A plain name that says what the buyer gets.
7. The offer written out the way a buyer will read it, short, and a test to run.

## Rules it must keep

- No fake urgency, fake scarcity, fake reviews, fake anchors.
- No guarantee the founder cannot honor.
- No claims (health, income, results) the founder cannot prove. Regulated claims get a
  "check with a lawyer" note.
- Not legal advice: refund rules and auto-renewal rules vary by state and country.

## Hand off

Writes `founder/offer.md`. Jake's founder-plan reads it. If the price or contents change,
the numbers change: point to founder-cfo and founder-pricing. Marketing reads the offer
for its message. Launch uses it on launch day.

## How we will know it beats the baseline

Same three cases: objections traced to sources, each offer piece tied to an objection and
costed, honest urgency, an affordable guarantee, and refusal of fake scarcity.
