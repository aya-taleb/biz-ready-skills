# Job spec: ops

Written before looking at any other operations skill.

## The job

Before opening, a founder has to know how the business runs on an ordinary Tuesday: who
supplies what, what happens in what order every day, which tools they need, which permits
and registrations apply, what could go wrong, and what all of that really costs. Most
first plans miss costs (insurance, card fees, packaging, software, a backup) and that is
where the margin disappears.

## What a good result has

1. Suppliers: what is needed, two or three options each with a link, price and lead time
   from their page or a quote, minimums and terms. Unknown prices are placeholders.
2. Routines: the daily and weekly run of the business, step by step, with times and who
   does it. Written so someone else could follow it.
3. Tools: the few that are needed now, with price from the vendor's page, and free options.
4. Permits and registrations to check: listed by level (federal, state, county, city), with
   the official page for each, and marked "to confirm" because they depend on the exact
   place and activity. Lead times as estimates to confirm.
5. A risk register: what could go wrong, how likely, how bad, the early sign, what to do
   now to prevent it, what to do if it happens.
6. Real costs written back to the CFO numbers: variable costs per unit, fixed monthly,
   startup, each with its source, in the shape Jake's founder-cfo reads, and a rerun.
7. Lead times handed to launch so the countdown uses them.

## Rules it must keep

- No invented prices, lead times or permit rules. Link the official page; else to confirm.
- Not legal, tax or insurance advice. Name who confirms each (the county, a licensed
  agent, an accountant).
- Never sign up for tools or contact suppliers for the user. Draft the quote requests.

## Hand off

Writes `founder/ops.md` and updates `founder/numbers.json` (with a backup of the old one)
and `founder/cfo-sources.md`. Jake's founder-cfo reruns from the new numbers; launch reads
lead times; brand reads what can be promised.

## How we will know it beats the baseline

Same three cases: sourced suppliers and tools, permits linked and marked to confirm, a
risk register with early signs, and real costs written back in the CFO's format with a
backup and a rerun.
