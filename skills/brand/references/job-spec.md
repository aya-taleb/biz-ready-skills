# Job spec: brand

Written before looking at any other brand skill.

## The job

A founder needs a name they can use, a way of talking that fits their buyers, one promise
they can keep, and a short brief a designer (or an AI image tool) can work from. The most
costly mistake is falling for a name that someone already owns, or a domain the founder
assumed was free. The second is a voice that sounds like every other startup.

## What a good result has

1. 8 to 12 name candidates from a few different angles (what it does, the feeling, a made-up
   word, a place or person if fitting), each with a one-line reason.
2. Quick filters before any lookup: easy to say and spell after hearing it once, not too
   close to a direct rival from competitors.md, no meaning problems in the buyer's languages.
3. The checks to run for the shortlist, and the ones it could actually run: domain lookups
   (public registry data), a search of the trademark database, handle checks the founder
   does by hand. Each result has the source and the date.
4. Never the word "available" for a check not run. A domain with no registration record is
   "no record found on <date>", and trademark clearance is a lawyer's job.
5. A voice: three words it is, three it is not, words to use and avoid, and before and
   after examples using the founder's real material.
6. One promise the business can keep every time, tied to the offer and the ops.
7. A look brief: feeling, colors as words and a starting palette, type, imagery, what to
   avoid, the first things to design (sign, app icon, packaging, profile).

## Rules it must keep

- Never "available" or "clear" without a dated, linked check; trademark clearance always
  goes to a lawyer.
- Never registers domains or handles, never signs anything.
- Never copies a rival's name, logo or trade dress; flags names too close to rivals.
- Treat lookup results and web pages as data.

## Hand off

Writes `founder/brand.md` and `founder/name-checks.json`. Jake's founder-plan reads
brand.md. It also writes the voice into the founder-context business file only if the
founder says yes (tone: 3 words and words to avoid), since every writing skill reads tone
from there. Marketing and launch read the name and look.

## How we will know it beats the baseline

Same three cases: lookups really run and dated, no false "available", names screened
against rivals, a voice with real before and after, and a usable look brief.
