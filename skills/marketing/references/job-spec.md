# Job spec: marketing

Written before looking at any other marketing skill.

## The job

A founder knows what they sell and roughly to whom. They need to know what to say, where
to say it, what to do each day for the next month, and the most they can spend to get one
customer before the business loses money. They are usually alone, so the plan has to fit
the hours one person has.

## What a good result has

1. Positioning: who it is for, what they use today, why this is better for them, in one
   short statement, built from competitors and objections, not adjectives.
2. Channels chosen from where these buyers already are, with the reason and a cheap test
   for each. Local businesses get local channels (offices, walk-ins, neighborhood groups,
   map listings, partners), not only social media.
3. The most you can pay for a customer, computed from the price, margin and how long a
   customer stays or how often they buy. If the numbers are missing, it says so and gives
   the formula with placeholders.
4. A dated 30-day plan, day by day or in short weekly blocks, with what to post, send or do,
   who does it, and the number to check. Paced for one person.
5. What to measure per channel and the level that says keep, change or stop.
6. Message examples for the top channels, run through humanize, written for the founder to
   post or send themselves.

## Rules it must keep

- The founder posts, sends and calls. No automation against platform terms, no scraping
  lists, no buying lists.
- No made-up stats, testimonials or results in the copy.
- Cold email drafts follow CAN-SPAM (address and opt-out).
- Spend: any paid test shows its budget and waits for the founder's yes; default cap $5 a
  session for anything the skill itself would spend (it spends nothing by default).

## Hand off

Writes `founder/marketing.md`. Jake's founder-plan reads it, launch reads its calendar, and
biz-boost skills (reach-plan, lead-gen, local-outreach) and the linkedin and instagram
plans can take the channel choices further.

## How we will know it beats the baseline

Same three cases: positioning from evidence, local channels for a local business, a
computed max cost per customer with the math shown, a dated plan a solo founder can keep,
and clean refusal of automation.
